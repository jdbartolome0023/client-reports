ReadThe gap is narrowing sharply in ratio terms, but it's still large. Meta's overstatement improved from ~35.8× in the previous period to 9.3× this month. That's real, continued progress on measurement, but the direction hasn't changed: Meta's own reported ROAS still can't be used for budget or creative decisions. The blended real cost per verified purchase this month is €240.03 (€4,560.61 spend ÷ 19 WooCommerce-verified orders), against Meta's own claimed CPA of €36.48, a very different number to plan a budget around.
Why this gap existsThis is an overstatement problem, not a missing-events problem. Meta's pixel is actively receiving and recording events, it just can't distinguish genuine purchases from other signals for consent-declined visitors, so it reports too many. Permanent, not a bug, and not solvable by CAPI either: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules (Graphedia confirmed Jul 7). CAPI (see Going Into Next Period) only improves match quality for visitors who already consented, it cannot recover anyone who declined, that part of the gap has no technical fix. This isn't purely consent-decline either, it includes unrelated non-Meta traffic too: only 19 of 447 total site orders this period carry any Meta or paid-social attribution tag at all. The verified figure is also a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is itself consent-gated, so some genuine Meta-driven purchases may be mistagged as Direct or Organic. The true number sits somewhere between the two lines above, closer to the verified side.

ReadOn real numbers, this month's loss was larger than last period's, in absolute euros, despite verified revenue growing more than 8x. On Meta's own claimed numbers it looks comfortably profitable (+€39,642.09). The honest answer to "was this month profitable" is: on Meta's dashboard, clearly yes; on what WooCommerce can independently verify, no, and the gap between the two pictures is still enormous.
Why the loss widened even as verification improvedSpend grew faster than verified revenue could catch up from its very small base. Spend rose 50.9% (€3,021.92 → €4,560.61) while verified revenue, though up 710% in percentage terms, only grew from €589.46 to €4,778.33 in cash terms. A near-8x improvement on a tiny base still isn't enough to outrun a 51% spend increase. Both sides of this loss figure carry a caveat, not just Meta's: the gap is permanent, not a bug, but the €4,778.33 "verified" figure is itself a floor, not an exact count, since WooCommerce's own attribution engine is consent-gated the same way Meta's pixel is. The real loss is very likely somewhere between this floor and Meta's claimed profit, but far closer to the loss shown here than to Meta's number.
Is this gap fixable?There's a real lever in motion, but I don't expect this gap to close completely. That's structural to how Meta's own attribution works, not a sign nothing's being done about it.

What I've already tried or ruled out: asking Niall to capture click IDs for visitors who decline cookies, so their later purchases could still be matched. He correctly declined this Jul 7 on GDPR/ePrivacy grounds, and I agree with that call, it's not something to revisit. I also looked at reclassifying WooCommerce's own order-attribution engine so it wouldn't need cookie consent either, then dropped that idea (Jul 15): doing that would very likely be the actual privacy violation, since attribution isn't required to complete a checkout.

CAPI (Conversions API) is the one real lever still in motion. I sent Niall everything he needs to build it on Jul 29 and followed up again this week, no confirmation yet that it's live. Once it lands, it should improve how many consented visitors' purchases Meta can actually match, narrowing the gap somewhat. A second lever, shortening how long Meta credits a click before counting a sale, is ready to consider once CAPI is confirmed live, not before: changing both at once would make it impossible to tell which change actually helped.

Even with CAPI working perfectly, I don't expect this to reach zero. Meta counts view-through and multi-touch credit across a wide window, a fundamentally different measurement than "this specific order happened because of this specific ad." Every ad platform's self-reported number runs hot against verified revenue for that reason, it's not unique to this account, just worse here because of the consent gap on top of it.

What I'm doing about it right now, rather than waiting on a fix: every figure in this report already shows both a floor (your real WooCommerce orders) and a ceiling (what Meta claims), instead of picking one number to trust. I make every budget or creative call off the verified floor, not Meta's own ROAS. Compost is the clearest example this period: Meta's own dashboard shows it as the one campaign moving the wrong way, but real verified orders actually grew, so I'm not treating it as a problem based on Meta's number alone.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (ex-VAT, ex-shipping), 447 completed orders Jul 1–31, 362 May 31–Jun 30. Both periods span exactly 31 calendar days. Refunded orders (7-8 per period) excluded entirely: WooCommerce's export lists each as two rows (an original-sale row and a separate negative reversal row), so including them would have double-counted rather than netting to zero.
  • Channel attribution: orders tagged "Source: [DV]..." (campaign-name tags), "Source: Paid+Social", "Source: Fb"/"Ig", or a Facebook/Instagram referral domain counted as Meta-verified. Organic, Direct, and other referral traffic excluded, even where it happens to mention Meta or Facebook.
  • Blended cost per purchase: spend ÷ WooCommerce-verified order count (€240.03 this period, €1,007.31 previous), not total site orders and not Meta's own reported purchase count. This is the number that actually reflects what a Meta-driven sale costs.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked by any method under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this specific piece, including CAPI. CAPI (Conversions API, still in progress with Graphedia) only improves match quality for visitors who already consented, it's a separate, smaller improvement, not a resolution to the consent-decline gap.
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Jul 1–31 / May 31–Jun 30 windows.
