ReadThe gap is narrowing in ratio terms, but not going away. Meta's overstatement improved from ~174× in June to 10.6× this period. That's real progress on measurement, but the direction hasn't changed: Meta's own reported ROAS still can't be used for budget or creative decisions. CAPI should narrow this further once live, but won't close it fully, the consent-declined share of the gap has no technical fix at all.
Why this gap existsThis is an overstatement problem, not a missing-events problem. Meta's pixel is actively receiving and recording events, it just can't distinguish genuine purchases from other signals for consent-declined visitors, so it reports too many. Permanent, not a bug, and not solvable by CAPI either: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules (Graphedia confirmed Jul 7). CAPI (see Going Into Next Period) only improves match quality for visitors who already consented, it cannot recover anyone who declined, that part of the gap has no technical fix. This isn't purely consent-decline either, it includes unrelated non-Meta traffic too: only 13 of 397 total site orders this period carry any Meta or paid-social attribution tag at all. The verified figure is also a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is itself consent-gated, so some genuine Meta-driven purchases may be mistagged as Direct or Organic. The true number sits somewhere between the two lines above, closer to the verified side.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (ex-VAT, ex-shipping), 397 valid orders Jul 1–26, 298 Jun 1–26. Both periods verified to cover all 26 calendar days with no gaps.
  • Channel attribution: orders tagged "Source: [DV]..." (campaign-name tags) or "Source: Paid+Social" counted as Meta-verified. Organic, Direct, and other referral traffic excluded, even where it happens to mention Meta or Facebook.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked by any method under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this specific piece, including CAPI. CAPI (Conversions API, still in progress with Graphedia) only improves match quality for visitors who already consented, it's a separate, smaller improvement, not a resolution to the consent-decline gap.
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Jul 1–26 / Jun 1–26 windows.
