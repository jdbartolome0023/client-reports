The decision this period

ReadOn September 4, Meta spend was deliberately cut from around €150/day to around €13/day. A 30-day check of real WooCommerce orders against Meta spend showed Meta running at roughly €150/day and producing about 23 genuinely Meta-attributed orders a month, worth around €9,200 net, against roughly €4,600 of spend. On a last-click basis that is a real loss of about €3,200 a month. Every paused category was checked against how well Google covers it: heating oil and gas are covered comprehensively by Google, firewood is covered by Google plus the one Meta campaign kept running, and compost and sand are small and organic-led. Six of the seven campaigns are now paused. Only Firewood B2B stays live, at €13/day, because it is the one campaign with a real, traceable return. This is a three-week test, not a permanent verdict: if a category's total revenue drops once Meta is off, that tells us Meta was contributing something the tracking couldn't see, and it gets rebuilt deliberately. Read date: September 25.

Why this gap existsThis is an overstatement problem, not a missing-events problem. Meta's pixel is receiving events, it just can't tell genuine purchases from other signals for consent-declined visitors. The gap is unusually wide this period because the Compost campaign alone claimed €11,533 of value on €55 of spend, almost all of it view-through (someone saw an ad, didn't click, bought later anyway). Partly permanent, not fully solvable even by the Conversions API: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish ePrivacy/GDPR rules. The verified figure is also a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution is consent-gated too, so some genuine Meta-driven purchases may sit mistagged as Direct, Organic, or another channel entirely, not only consent-declined ones.
Full contextTotal website sales for the period, all sources, were €57,747.03 across 136 orders. Of that, €856.38 (2 orders) is verified as Meta-driven, both from new customers. Cost per verified Meta order this period was €307.93 (€615.86 ÷ 2), against €233.29 in Aug 1–6 (€933.17 ÷ 4, 3 new / 1 returning). This blended cost-per-order, not Meta's own reported figure, is the one to judge against category margins.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" (ex-VAT, ex-shipping). Meta-attributed = orders tagged "Source: Paid Social", "Source: Ig/Fb", or a Meta campaign name, in the WooCommerce Attribution field. 2 such orders Sep 1–6, 4 Aug 1–6.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under GDPR/ePrivacy rules. No fix exists for this piece, including the Conversions API, which only improves match quality for visitors who already consented.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine (Sourcebuster JS) is also gated behind cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • Meta's reported value: includes view-through conversions (ad seen, not clicked, purchase made later) and cannot be reconciled to real orders on this account. Shown as a ceiling only.
  • Category margins: supplied by you (firewood 25%, heating oil 10%). Real net uses these against verified orders only.
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting API for the exact Sep 1–6 / Aug 1–6 windows. Full method: reporting-methodology.html.
