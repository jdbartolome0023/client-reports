Turned off Aug 17, see Recommendations.

Recommendations
AI Max broad-match leak found and fixed on Brand Search
Brand Search's cost-per-conversion nearly tripled period over period (€13.05 to €29.92) while every other campaign held flat or improved - traced to an AI Max setting ("Branded searches: show ads on all relevant searches") that was matching the campaign to competitor names and generic price-shopper queries, not just Kenny Fuels' own brand. Turned off entirely Aug 17 (the core "kenny fuels" keyword is already Quality Score 10, so AI Max's ad-copy/reach features had little upside against this real downside). 34 negative keywords also pushed live as interim coverage. Recheck the search-terms report in ~1 week to confirm the leak has actually closed.
Real net loss on Google, mostly a comparison-baseline artifact, same finding as Aug 1-9 confirmed on a wider window
Real revenue fell 61.9% against a 1.3% spend increase. Root cause: an unusual 4-day demand spike (Jul 8, 9, 10, 13) inside the Jul 1-16 comparison window, consistent with a heating-oil bulk-fill pattern, accounts for most of the swing - normalized daily real revenue was roughly flat (-5.7%) once excluded. No further urgent action on the comparison itself; the AI Max fix above is the concrete lever for what remains.
Firewood's platform ROAS looked like it collapsed - it didn't, real revenue is stable
Platform-reported ROAS dropped to 2.71x this period from a historical 24.9x-35.8x range, which would normally be a red flag - but real WooCommerce revenue for Firewood actually held steady (€2,224 Aug vs €2,057 Jul, real average order value €230-370). The campaign was running on the account's flat €90 default conversion value the whole time, a blind spot Brand Search and Home Heating Oil already had fixed via a multiplier rule. Built and pushed the equivalent fix for Firewood (x3.17 multiplier) Aug 17 - future reports should show this campaign's platform ROAS tracking much closer to real performance.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (ex-VAT, ex-shipping), 176 valid orders Aug 1–16, 277 Jul 1–16 (refunded orders excluded from both counts).
  • Channel attribution: orders tagged exactly "Source: Google" counted as Google-verified. "Organic: Google" (unpaid search) is never summed into this total, under any framing, since organic traffic has nothing to do with Google Ads spend.
  • Standing exclusion: Compost, Sand+Stones, and Coal/solid fuel revenue tagged "Source: Google" is excluded from Google's real-revenue total: no Google Ads campaign targets any of these categories, so this is confirmed misattribution.
  • This period's gap is a volume story, not a tagging story: the Jul 8 UTM/auto-tagging fix remains in place and working. The gap this period traces to a real comparison-baseline anomaly (Jul demand spike) plus a real, separate issue (the AI Max leak), not a measurement regression.
  • Category fingerprinting: blank-Product(s) orders matched to a named category only where corroborated by a signal (the `kenny10` coupon code for Heating Oil). Price-range matching alone was deliberately not used this period: Firewood's real order-value range fully contains Heating Oil's, a known issue that produces false matches if trusted without corroboration.
  • Kenergy Solar: excluded from all real-profit math and every Google real-vs-platform ratio. It's a quote-based install service, not a WooCommerce checkout product, so it has zero orders to verify against. Its €12,000-per-conversion ADD rule was paused Aug 13 pending a real closed-deal confirmation.
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Google Ads' own reporting API (GAQL) for the exact Aug 1–16 / Jul 1–16 windows.
