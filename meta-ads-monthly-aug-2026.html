ReadThe gap is essentially unchanged, sitting inside the same 10 percent real-versus-platform band this account has shown since mid-July. Real verified revenue grew 88.4 percent month over month (€4,778.33 to €8,997.71), faster than the platform figure, but not enough yet to move the headline multiple. No sign yet that the Conversions API (confirmed live August 10) has narrowed the overstatement.
Full contextKenny Fuels took €201,767 in website sales across all channels in August (531 orders), up from €174,046 in July (447 orders). Of August's total, €8,997.71 is independently WooCommerce-attributed to Meta (the verified floor above), against €91,315.77 that Meta's own dashboard claims for the same period. The bulk of website revenue is Heating Oil ordered direct or through the Google channel, not Meta.
Why this gap existsThis is an overstatement problem, not a missing-events problem. Meta's pixel is actively receiving and recording events, it just can't distinguish genuine purchases from other signals for visitors who declined marketing cookies, so it reports too many. Partly permanent, not fully solvable even by the Conversions API: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked for ad attribution under Irish privacy rules. The Conversions API only improves match quality for visitors who already consented. The verified figure is also a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is itself consent-gated, so some genuine Meta-driven purchases may be mistagged as Direct or Organic, which is why every figure above is shown as a floor-to-ceiling range rather than one number.

The real loss is very likely somewhere between the floor shown here and Meta's claimed profit, but far closer to the loss shown here.

Methodology & caveats

  • Real revenue basis: WooCommerce "Net Sales" column (ex-VAT, ex-shipping), 531 valid orders Aug 1–31, 447 Jul 1–31 (refunded orders excluded from both counts).
  • Channel attribution: orders tagged with a campaign-name tag, "Source: Paid+Social", "Source: Ig", "Source: Fb", or a facebook.com referral counted as Meta-verified. Organic, Direct, and other referral traffic excluded, even where it happens to mention Meta or Facebook.
  • Permanent limitation: visitors who decline marketing cookies cannot legally be tracked by any method under Irish privacy rules. No fix exists for this specific piece, including the Conversions API. The Conversions API (confirmed live August 10) only improves match quality for visitors who already consented, a separate, smaller improvement, not a resolution to the consent-decline gap.
  • The verified figure is a floor, not an exact count: WooCommerce's own attribution engine is itself gated behind statistics cookie consent, so some genuinely Meta-driven purchases land as Direct or Organic instead.
  • One honest caveat: about a third of orders don't carry product details in the export, so category-level margin figures carry some real uncertainty. The channel split (Google vs Meta vs Direct) is solid regardless.
  • July comparison: both months are recomputed with the current classification method so the comparison is like for like.
  • Live platform data: pulled fresh via Meta's own reporting interface for the exact Aug 1–31 and Jul 1–31 windows. ROAS means return on ad spend. CPA means cost per purchase.
  • Full method: how attribution, verification, and the floor-to-ceiling range are calculated across every report is on the reporting methodology page.
